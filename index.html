How to Use Pitch Predictor
1
Set up your pitcher. Tap the Pitcher card or the gear button to open Settings. Enter your pitcher's name, handedness, mound distance, and release height/extension. Then fill in the averages table: Velo, Vert (IVB), and Horz for each pitch he throws. Or press Load Sample Pitcher to see how it works.
2
Pick the batter. Tap one of the 9 preset batters, or choose Custom to build a profile from scratch. For a custom batter, set his handedness, tap where he does damage (the hot zone picker), toggle any scouting notes that apply, and write a memo. Press Save to store him for next time, then click his chip to reload him instantly when he's up again.
3
Set the count. Use the + and − buttons for Balls and Strikes. Or just use the What Happened buttons (Strike, Ball, Foul, Whiff, Out, Hit) after each pitch, they update the count for you. Press First Pitch to jump to 0-0 and get an opening call driven by the batter's profile.
4
Mark the pitch you just threw. Tap the pitch type, set the velocity slider, and tap the zone grid to show where it went. Then set the hitter's timing (Early, Late, On Time, or Unknown). The engine uses all of this to pick what to throw next.
5
Press GO. The engine ranks your top 5 pitches by score, with the location and whether it's a Strike or Chase pitch. The Predicted Spot map shows numbered dots where each pitch should go. Tap any recommendation to see why it ranked there, every scoring factor and point value, plus what the pitch is designed to do.
6
Check the Engine Reading. The panel below the Predicted Spot map shows everything the engine is working with: the batter's profile, the last pitch data (including flight time and perceived velocity from the physics engine), and the current matchup.
The engine is a deterministic calculator, not an AI. It uses real ball-flight physics (flight time, commit-point tunneling, vertical approach angle, perceived velocity) plus your inputs to score every pitch. Same inputs always give the same recommendation.
